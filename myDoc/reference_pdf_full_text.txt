3. Grad-CAM: how it was used and what it showed
How Grad-CAM works
Grad-CAM takes the gradient of a class score with respect to the feature maps of the last convolutional
layer, averages each gradient into a channel weight, adds up the weighted feature maps and keeps the
positive part. The result is a coarse heatmap (7 x 7 for a 224 px input, enlarged to image size) showing
which regions pushed the model toward that class. It shows what drives a prediction. It does not prove
those regions are disease lesions.
What the notebook did
 - Library: pytorch-grad-cam. Layer: last block of layer4 for ResNet50, last feature block for EfficientNet-B0
and MobileNetV2.
- The same random sample of 150 test images for all three models.
- For each image, the target class is the class the model predicted on the clean image. The heatmap on
the clean image is compared with the heatmap for the same class on each of 8 corrupted versions
(severity 2 and 3 of blur, dark, bright, JPEG).
- Three similarity scores: Pearson correlation, SSIM, and overlap (IoU) of the top 20% pixels. 1 means
identical.
- Cross-model agreement: the clean heatmaps of two models compared on the 149 images that all three
classify correctly.

the idea that models whose evidence moves less also fail less.

below confidence in all 10 usable settings (see the next section).

4. Wording you can adapt
Methods: models
 We compared three ImageNet-pretrained convolutional networks (ResNet50, EfficientNet-B0 and MobileNetV2) for
five-class coconut disease classification. All models were fine-tuned end to end under identical conditions: the
same grouped train/validation/test split, 224 x 224 input, the same augmentation, AdamW with a cosine schedule
for 15 epochs, and class-weighted cross-entropy to handle class imbalance. The checkpoint with the best
validation macro-F1 was kept.
Methods: Grad-CAM
 We used Grad-CAM on the last convolutional block of each network to visualize which regions support a
prediction. For 150 test images, the heatmap of the clean-image predicted class was compared with the heatmap
of the same class on eight corrupted versions of the image, using Pearson correlation, SSIM and the overlap of the
top 20% of pixels. Cross-model agreement was measured on images that all three models classified correctly.
Grad-CAM maps are coarse (7 x 7) and show what drives a prediction; they were not validated against expert
lesion annotations.
Results: robustness
 All three models were almost perfect on clean images (macro-F1 0.997-0.998, overlapping confidence intervals),
so clean accuracy did not separate them. Under severe blur, performance collapsed (macro-F1 0.14-0.42)
because most images were assigned to one or two classes; the collapse class differed between models.
Confidence-based rejection did not rescue blurred inputs, and temperature scaling reduced calibration error only
partly and cannot change predicted classes.
Results: explanations
 Grad-CAM explanations were stable under brightening and mild darkening (Pearson 0.83-0.97) and degraded
under severe blur (0.10-0.49), the same condition in which predictions collapsed. Cross-model heatmap
agreement was moderate (Pearson 0.70-0.75). Explanation instability was a weaker error signal than prediction
confidence and added little when combined with it.
Limitations
 Results come from one public dataset and one training seed per model, so model rankings are not reliable. The
corruptions are synthetic. Grad-CAM maps are coarse and were not compared with expert annotations, so they do
not prove that the models attend to disease symptoms.
Ready-to-use slide points
 - Same split, same training, three models: clean accuracy cannot tell them apart.
- Failure is a cliff: harmless up to moderate damage, collapse under heavy blur.
- Under blur, models pick one "sink" class and stay confident.
- Grad-CAM: models mostly look at the diseased plant part, but maps are coarse.
- Explanations become unstable exactly when predictions fail.
- Next: stricter duplicate-free split, extra seeds, blur gate, external photos.
Do not claim yet
 - Any model ranking (it changed between runs).
- That one disease is the most vulnerable (the pattern follows each model's sink class).
- That the heatmaps prove the models use lesions.
- The clean 0.998 as a generalization figure (the split still had near-copies).
